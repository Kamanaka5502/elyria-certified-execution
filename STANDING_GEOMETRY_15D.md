## Core rule

```text
Possibility is not standing.
Execution is not proof.
A record is not replay.
Consequence does not bind until standing geometry resolves.
```

## 15D standing model

The public standing geometry includes fifteen visible dimensions:

```text
D01 Authority
D02 Custody
D03 Evidence
D04 Capacity
D05 Policy
D06 Continuity
D07 Revocation
D08 State
D09 Risk
D10 Failure Behavior
D11 Receipt Basis
D12 Replay Basis
D13 Thermo-Standing
D14 Corridor Standing
D15 Standing Decay
```

## Dimension definitions

### D01 Authority

The actor, system, agent, or process must have valid current authority for the proposed consequence.

### D02 Custody

The evidence, input, state, and action path must preserve custody sufficient for the consequence being evaluated.

### D03 Evidence

The decision must be grounded in evidence that is present, current enough, scoped, and admissible for the intended use.

### D04 Capacity

The system must have sufficient capacity to carry the consequence without borrowing coherence from external correction, override, or hidden debt.

### D05 Policy

The proposed consequence must satisfy the governing policy basis currently in force.

### D06 Continuity

The action must remain continuous with the lawful path that admits it; prior approval alone is not enough.

### D07 Revocation

The system must account for current revocation state before consequence binds.

### D08 State

The current state must be proven. Unprovable state is not degraded validity; it blocks binding consequence.

### D09 Risk

Risk must be bounded within the admissible range for the current authority, evidence, state, and consequence.

### D10 Failure Behavior

The system must fail closed when standing cannot be proven.

### D11 Receipt Basis

The decision must bind to structured evidence sufficient to prove what was admitted, refused, narrowed, escalated, halted, quarantined, or rebounded.

### D12 Replay Basis

The decision must be reproducible under the same state, input, and governing basis.

### D13 Thermo-Standing

The system must remain within capacity, burden, and coherence limits rather than continuing by accumulating hidden instability.

### D14 Corridor Standing

If consequence crosses a boundary, the transfer relation itself must have standing. Valid endpoints do not prove valid transfer.

### D15 Standing Decay

Standing can degrade before visible collapse. The system must detect loss of standing before failure becomes externally visible.
